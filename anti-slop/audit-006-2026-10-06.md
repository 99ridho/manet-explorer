## Copywriting checklist

- PASS, no fabricated numbers. Every number in a story is one of three kinds. Some are computed by the app and pinned in an `operations.test.ts`: M2 reaches M3 in 3 hops, B runs out at packet 20, M is reported after its fourth miss. Some are quoted from a reference with its page: Misra pp. 338-339, Loo p. 203, Misra Definition 1.4. The rest are demo values the story labels as this demo's: the 16 addresses, q = 0.8, the radius of 2, the watchdog threshold of 3, the area and speeds of Week 5, and the example link values of Week 7. The one percentage in a why, 25 %, is the computed area share the step compares against.
- PASS, no buzzwords. A grep of every new `story.ts`, `why.ts`, and the glossary for unlock, seamless, powerful, robust, journey, and cutting-edge finds nothing.
- PASS, no em dashes. `npm run lint:copy` passed, and `explain.test.ts` checks every Why line for the em dash and the arrow glyph.
- PASS, every sentence names its actor. Why lines name the radio, sensor, laptop, or tent that acts ("C splits its range", "M pulled the route toward itself on purpose"). The stories name roles, not invented people.
- PASS, no AI-rhythm tells. I reread each story and a sample of Why lines from every topic aloud. Each why states one reason and at most its consequence. Lists hold the count their content has: four steps in one Try this list, six in another.
- PASS, quotes only where they carry meaning. The stories quote no one; the glossary quotes the AREQ question once, as the message's content.
- PASS, every story is labeled. Each topic scenario opens with "*Illustrative scenario*" and says the slides do not describe that setting; `explain.test.ts` checks the label on every topic and case study.
- PASS, claims trace to the references. Before writing each story I reread its week's reference, and every book fact in a story or a glossary definition carries the page that reference gives.
