# antislop audit 007, 2026-10-16

Mode: DURING. Scope: the ns-3 tab that replaced the Protocol tab (SPEC §7.3, ADR-020). That is eleven ns-3 scripts in `ns3/` with their comments and captured output, the notes in each topic's `ns3.ts`, `Ns3Panel` and its labels, the "This demo's choices" sections added to eleven Scenario tabs, the Start page line about the tab, and the SPEC, ADR, CLAUDE.md, and README text that records them.

## Copywriting checklist

- PASS, no fabricated numbers. Every number in a note is one of three kinds. Some come from a captured ns-3 run that `ns3.test.ts` checks: 3 transmissions under MPR relaying, the path A, B, C, E, C keeping 13 to 14. Some are seed values the visualizer pins: S, A, C, D, then S, B, E, D. The rest are demo values the notes or comments label as this demo's: 50 m per unit, the 4 dB fade, 5 J per battery unit, the 10 ms relay jitter, 3 AREQ tries. The "This demo's choices" sections reuse the reviewed sentences of the deleted `structure.ts` files with their pages (Misra pp. 31-32, p. 125, pp. 338-339, p. 241, pp. 444-445).
- PASS, no buzzwords. A grep of the scripts, the notes, `Ns3Panel`, the stories, and the Start page for unlock, seamless, powerful, robust, journey, cutting-edge, leverage, elevate, delve, and effortless finds nothing.
- PASS, no em dashes. `npm run lint:copy` now scans `ns3/` and `.cc` and `.txt` files too, and passed; `ns3.test.ts` also checks every script, note, and output for the em dash and the arrow glyph.
- PASS, every sentence names its actor. The notes name the node, the protocol, or ns-3 that acts ("M answers at once", "ns-3's DSDV always sends both kinds of update", "the MAC retries what it loses").
- PASS, no AI-rhythm tells. I read every note aloud. Each has one paragraph on what the script does, a list with one line per variant where the variants behave differently, and one paragraph on where ns-3 differs. A note leaves out a part it has nothing for.
- PASS, quotes only where they carry meaning. The notes quote nothing; command lines appear as code.
- PASS, comments follow `antislop-code`. Script comments say what ns-3 does or why a value was chosen, one or two lines each: why a link loss matrix stands in for a range, why messages repeat, why a call leaves a callback. No banners, no step narration, no TODOs.
- PASS, no claim about the course beyond the references. The only course claim in the scripts and notes beyond the reused sentences is the Week 6 note naming ns-3 (Loo p. 66), quoted in the ADR and SPEC. Statements about ns-3 describe what the pinned release's source does, and I read that source for each one (the range model's `<=`, AODV's `TtlStart`, DSR's ICMP hook, the energy model's switch-off event).
